    Where the cost sits (assumed split)

    An illustration, not AgiBot data. Joint modules 60%, compute 12%, cameras and lidar 8%, battery 8%, frame 7%, harness 3%, other 2%. There is no public bill of materials.

    Worked example: US joints alone reach about 60% (short). Add a US frame and harness and the example reaches about 70%, which clears 2028 and fails 2029. Add a US-built pack and carrier (optimistic, the open subassembly question) and it reaches about 82%.

    Constraints, side by side

    RuleWhat it restrictsWhat it does not doBuild impact
    FCC Covered ListDA 26-786, July 28, 2026 New FCC authorization for foreign-produced advanced robotic devices. To count as domestic, the robot must be manufactured in the US, and domestic components must be more than 65% of component cost through 2028 and more than 75% from 2029 (48 CFR 25.101(a)). Does not look at software origin or company nationality. Does not ban the components themselves. The COTS end-product waiver does not apply. Sales only to the government are exempt. Gatekeeper Drives every sourcing choice on this site.
    Buy American valuation48 CFR 25.003 How component cost is measured. A component is an article built directly into the end product. Bought parts count at acquisition cost plus freight plus duty. Parts you make count at manufacturing cost plus allocable overhead, with no profit. Final assembly labor does not count. A component of unknown origin is not given the benefit of the doubt: it counts as foreign. Method A purchased joint module is wholly domestic or wholly foreign.
    ITAR / EARexport controls Exports and releases of controlled items and technology. Possible controls: 2B006.c encoders (machine-tool, about 0.9 arc-second or better), 7A003/7A103 navigation-grade IMUs, ITAR for parts specially designed for defense, and 2B007 robots. Typical joint actuators, harmonic drives, commercial torque sensors, and robot-balance IMUs are usually EAR99. For the Jetson Orin, check NVIDIA's published classification. Watch Sending drawings or firmware to a Chinese supplier can be an export.
    Section 232robotics & industrial machinery An investigation into imports of robotics and industrial machinery, opened September 2, 2025. There is no final rule yet. It is not a license requirement today. Check status Pending; it could raise the cost of imported parts later. Chinese-origin parts may already carry Section 301 duties.
    Sanctioned partiesOFAC SDN list Do not buy from parties on the SDN list. It is not a general ban on Chinese or other foreign suppliers. Screen Screen every supplier before you buy.
    Assumptions
    • The robot on the Robot tab is a planning likeness of the AgiBot A2 Ultra-class form. It is not an official model and carries no AgiBot logos or wordmarks. The build planner's X2, A3, and D1 classes are not carried over to this site.
    • A2 / A2 Ultra figures come from AgiBot's own pages: 169 cm, about 69 kg, 40 DOF, a 16-core CPU plus Jetson Orin 64 GB, 14.4 Ah, a 54.6 V 8 A charger output, 2 h charge, and a 270 N·m peak knee torque. The 3-1-3 arm and 3-1-2 leg split of those DOF is a modeling assumption.
    • IMU, encoders, torque sensors, reducers, foot force sensors, BMS, and harness are typical humanoid items. They are not published A2 Ultra line items.
    • Cost shares are relative placeholders, not quotes and not AgiBot data. There is no public BOM for any platform.
    • Supplier names reported for AgiBot (Estun, Leaderdrive, CATL, BYD, Orbbec) are inferred by a third party and unverified. Every other pin on the globe is a candidate taken from the compliance notes, not a supplier under contract. Pin locations are headquarters or main-plant cities, placed for orientation; the place a specific unit is made decides its origin.
